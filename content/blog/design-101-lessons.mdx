When I designed my first websites, I thought the hard part would be making things *look* good. I was wrong. The hard part is making things *work* for the person using them.

## 1. Whitespace is not empty space

Some of my works are quite minimalist. At first I fought it — I wanted to fill every corner. Then I noticed: the designs that felt calm and premium were the ones that **left room to breathe**.

## 2. Consistency beats creativity (at first)

Using the same button, the same spacing scale, the same typography in every section makes a site feel *designed*. One-off flourishes everywhere make it feel *assembled*. Build a small system, then break it on purpose.

## 3. Design for the story

Whether it's an e-commerce page for a parfum label or a podcast home for storytellers, the questions stay the same:

- Who is this for?
- What should they feel?
- What should they do next?

Answer those, and the layout almost draws itself. The rest is polish — and polish, I'm still learning every day.
